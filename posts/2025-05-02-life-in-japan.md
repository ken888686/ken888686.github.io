# 🚀 Thoughts on Life: From Hungry & Foolish to The Quest for Spark

> Steve Jobs: "Stay Hungry, Stay Foolish."

## 😴 The Spark Fades

Back in my university days, I remember staying up late with classmates, eagerly following Apple's product and technology announcements. I can't help but feel how quickly time flies! Every time a new product was unveiled, I'd rush to the nearest Apple store the next day for a **first contact** :alien:. I desperately wanted to **cajole my parents** into buying it right away.

Now, as an adult with income and purchasing power, I find myself indifferent to these new things. I even feel they're nothing special. After all, I probably wouldn't use most of those features anyway—just scrolling through IG, messaging my wife on LINE, and watching random clips. My current iPhone 11 Pro :iphone: handles all of that perfectly fine. I have absolutely no motivation to upgrade to a new (and increasingly expensive) device.

The passion for my work is also slowly fading. I just want to get by each day, doing as little as possible. I wonder if anyone else feels this way. If you do, perhaps you can take a leaf out of my book on **coasting** through life.

---

## 🧑‍💻 Work Life Journey

### 🔰 The Beginning

Immediately after graduating and completing my military service in Chiayi, I moved to Taipei to look for a job, as it offered more opportunities for Software Engineers.

I was fortunate to land my first job at one of Taiwan's top Industrial PC companies. My daily routine involved working with the PMs to create corresponding Windows Images for each machine, handing the files over to QA for testing, and assisting other FAEs with customer issues. During this time, I met many colleagues from different teams, all of whom offered significant help. I especially want to thank my manager then (an alumna from the same university). To some other colleagues, she might have seemed difficult, but to me, she was the one who brought this fresh graduate into the company, tolerating my willfulness and immaturity. I owe her immense gratitude.

### 🧗 Seeking New Challenges

After two happy years there, the daily routine started making me feel stagnant—like there was no room for growth or challenge. As an energetic young man in my early twenties, I felt a restlessness stirring within. In my spare time, I would often visit various software or business exhibitions at Taipei Expo Park or World Trade Center to keep up with trends and broaden my horizons—a definite perk of working in Taipei.

Inspired by the dazzling concepts and products I saw, I decided to challenge these **new things** :new:. I resolutely submitted my resignation and moved to a small startup to embark on a completely new development journey.

### 📉 Startup to End

Unlike the meticulously structured division of labor in a large corporation, joining a startup of fewer than twenty people meant I had to be hands-on with almost everything and manage my own tasks well. This gave me an unprecedented work experience. Everyone worked closely together. I transitioned from Industrial PC development to becoming a Backend Engineer, working with `.NET Core Web API`—the technology I was eager to explore.

Although I sometimes spent significant time solving problems for certain features, I genuinely loved this technology, making the hard work worthwhile. Unfortunately, good times didn't last; by the end of the same year I joined, the company decided to file for bankruptcy. This left me—**happily, I suppose, to celebrate New Year's**—to seek my next opportunity.

### 🛠️ Continuing My Skills

The following January, I took a week off, and then began my job search. After several rounds of interviews with various companies, I joined the IT department of a large real estate agency. My job continued the experience I had accumulated at the startup: developing internal company systems using `.Net Core Web API`.

At the time, most of the company's systems were still on `.Net Framework 4.x`, and I was tasked with updating some existing projects. I must say, a large company has its scale and structure. Compared to the freedom of the previous startup, there were more established Design Patterns and development processes. I deepened my Web API development skills here. My manager and senior colleagues were truly impossible to stump—**though that might also be due to my limited expertise!** They provided excellent, clear explanations for any question, which was a huge eye-opener for me, proving the adage that there is always someone better out there :mountain: :mountain:.

### 💍 Pandemic - Covid 19

When the terrifying pandemic hit, governments had to lock down cities and restrict daily life to prevent further spread of the virus. At the time, I had a foreign girlfriend whom I was planning to marry. The pandemic forced us to change our wedding plans. We discussed it and decided to register the marriage first and hold the wedding later, flying to my then-fiancée's home country for the registration.

Due to Taiwanese law, my foreign fiancée needed to fly back to her home country to apply for a **Certificate of No Impediment to Marriage** before we could register in Taiwan. However, my certificate was not required in her country. Thus, we embarked on the journey of registering our cross-border marriage abroad. I won't detail the tedious paperwork and procedures, but in short, it was my **first time** traveling abroad alone, and under the severe conditions of the pandemic.

Upon arrival, the local government immediately sent us to a quarantine hotel for a fourteen-day isolation. After isolation, we quickly went through all the wedding procedures: suits, gowns, venue, photography, pastor, guests, and a whole host of complicated arrangements. I have to thank my wife; she truly is an incredibly capable partner who handled almost all the arrangements herself. As her husband, I naturally offered my full support, and with God's blessing, this cross-border marriage was completed.

Unsurprisingly, upon returning to Taiwan, we faced another fourteen days of quarantine. The funny thing was, we lived together abroad, took the same flight :airplane:, and the same taxi :taxi: to the quarantine hotels, but we were not allowed to isolate in the same room. So, we spent a lot of extra **money** :dollar: :dollar: to get through those fourteen days (**Seriously?!**). Finally, the journey was complete.

### 🗼 New Environment - Japan

My wife had been working for a Japanese company for nearly seven years and felt ready for a new challenge. After much discussion and thought, we decided to look for job opportunities in Japan while in Taiwan. Fortunately, we both found positions and promptly moved to Tokyo :tokyo_tower: to start our new life.

For her, living in Tokyo was already familiar. For me, it was the complete opposite—I had barely flown a few times, let alone lived abroad, and my Japanese was non-existent. Thankfully, I could somewhat guess meanings through the _kanji_. Before starting my job, I had a short period to handle basic living procedures and enrolled in a language school to begin learning Japanese from scratch. My wife, the absolute superhero :female_superhero: started work the day after we moved.

### 😫 Work Life in Japan

In the few short years since coming to Japan, I've already changed jobs three times and am currently on my fourth. Although each new job has come with a salary increase, I constantly find myself complaining, lamenting my lack of growth, and feeling dissatisfied with my current role or simply lacking the desire to work hard. I believe this has seriously affected my work ethic.

I want to share this advice: **Don't let yourself sink like I have.** You must constantly push and improve yourself. Here's a little story I often remind myself of:

> A 1000 NTD bill, whether it's in your wallet or in the trash, still retains its value of 1000 NTD.<br/>
> So, don't complain about a bad environment. Instead, reflect on whether you have inherent value. If you do, you'll be worth 1000 NTD no matter where you go.

### 📝 Summary

I am still working on adjusting my mindset. I took advantage of Golden Week to give myself a fresh start and stop complaining, which led to writing this entire piece of reflection and venting. I hope everyone avoids this downward spiral and lives each day, both at work and in life, fully and without regret.

> There may be regrets in life, but there must be no remorse.
